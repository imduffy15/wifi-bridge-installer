Put any wired device on Wi-Fi

WiFi Bridge turns a WT32-ETH01 or another ESP32 Ethernet board into a Wi-Fi client that bridges to its Ethernet port. Printers, NAS boxes, consoles and other wired-only devices join your wireless network without new cables.

This browser can't flash devices. Installing needs Web Serial, which is available in desktop Chrome, Edge and Opera. Open this page in one of those browsers.
This page must be served over HTTPS (or from localhost) to access serial ports.

Connect the board over USB first (see the steps below), then click Install and pick its serial port. On a first install, choose Erase device.

What it does

A small, dedicated bridge: one wired device on one side, your Wi-Fi network on the other.

Wi-Fi client to Ethernet

The board joins your Wi-Fi network as a client and forwards traffic to and from the device plugged into its Ethernet port.

Same LAN, same addresses

The wired device gets its address from your router's DHCP and is reachable from the rest of the network like any other client.

Web setup and OTA updates

Configure Wi-Fi from a setup page on your phone or laptop, watch status, and install updates from the browser without a cable.

Install

You need a computer with desktop Chrome, Edge or Opera, a USB cable or USB-UART adapter, and a couple of minutes.

  1. Connect the board

    Boards with USB (Olimex, ESP32-Ethernet-Kit): plug the board into your computer. The installer puts it into download mode for you.

    Olimex ESP32-POE (non-ISO): do not power it from PoE while it is connected to your computer by USB. Only the ISO variant is galvanically isolated.

    WT32-ETH01 has no USB port. Connect a USB-UART adapter set to 3.3 V logic:

    AdapterWT32-ETH01
    TXRX0
    RXTX0
    GNDGND
    5V5V (or 3.3 V to 3V3 if the adapter can supply 500 mA)
    GND (jumper)IO0, held to GND while the board powers up or resets

    The ESP32 only enters its bootloader if IO0 is low at reset. Connect IO0 to GND, then power the board on (or briefly short EN to GND). Leave the jumper in place until flashing finishes.

  2. Click Install

    Click Install WiFi Bridge at the top of this page and choose the serial port (often CP2102, CH340 or FT232). If the port is missing, install the driver for your adapter; on Linux, add your user to the dialout group.

  3. Erase and flash

    On a first install, or when replacing other firmware such as Tasmota or ESPHome, tick Erase device. This removes old settings that could confuse the bridge. Flashing takes about a minute.

  4. Restart the board

    On a WT32-ETH01, remove the IO0 jumper and power-cycle the board (or pulse EN). Boards with USB restart by themselves.

After flashing

A new bridge has no Wi-Fi settings, so it offers two ways in.

Over Wi-Fi

Join the setup network wifi-bridge-xxxxxx from a phone or laptop (the suffix comes from the board's MAC address). A captive portal opens; if it doesn't, browse to http://192.168.4.1.

Over Ethernet

Plug a computer into the board's Ethernet port. It gets an address by DHCP; browse to http://192.168.5.1 for the same setup page.

Then

Choose your Wi-Fi network, enter its password and, if needed, select your board type. The bridge joins your network; plug in the wired device and it appears on your LAN.

Updating

Which file do I use?
  • wifi-bridge-<version>.ota.bin: the application only. Upload it in the device's web interface, or through Tasmota safeboot. Use this for updates.
  • wifi-bridge-<version>.factory.bin: the complete image (bootloader, partition table, application) to flash at offset 0x0 over serial. This page installs it for you.
  • wifi-bridge-<version>.elf (on the GitHub release): debug symbols for decoding crash backtraces.
Flashing from the command line

With esptool installed and the board in download mode:

esptool.py --chip esp32 write_flash 0x0 wifi-bridge-<version>.factory.bin

Add erase_flash as a separate command first to start from a clean flash.

The installer can't connect to the board
  • Check that TX and RX are crossed (adapter TX to board RX0).
  • WT32-ETH01: make sure IO0 was connected to GND before the reset, and that the adapter uses 3.3 V logic.
  • Close other programs using the port, such as a serial monitor or another browser tab.
  • Try a shorter cable or a different USB port, and power the board from 5 V rather than the adapter's 3.3 V pin.
